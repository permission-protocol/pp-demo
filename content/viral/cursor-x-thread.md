# X Thread: Cursor Is Dead

## Tweet 1 (hook)
Cursor went from $0 to $29 billion to existential crisis in three years.

That's not a Cursor problem. That's the new speed of product death.

🧵

## Tweet 2 (timeline)
The speedrun:

2022 — Founded, nobody notices
2023 — Launched, $8M seed
Aug 2024 — $60M Series A, $400M valuation
Dec 2024 — $2.5 billion
Jun 2025 — $9.9 billion
Nov 2025 — $29.3 billion, $1B+ ARR

Peak. Confidence.

## Tweet 3 (the fall)
Then:

Jul 2025 — Pricing change, mass cancellations
Feb 2026 — Users calling Composer 1.5 "an absolute garbage slop machine"
Mar 2026 — Devs asking "Cursor or just... agents?"

The category Cursor created got eaten from below.

## Tweet 4 (the paradigm shift)
Cursor's bet: developers would always be the ones typing. The AI assists. You drive.

Then Codex and Claude Code showed up as standalone agents. No editor needed. You describe what you want, walk away, come back to a pull request.

Different paradigm. Category kill.

## Tweet 5 (the bigger insight)
But here's the real story:

Mainframes — decades
PCs — 20 years
Smartphones — 15 years
Web frameworks — 5-7 years
AI tools — months

Product lifecycles are compressing exponentially. Cursor is just the most visible casualty so far.

## Tweet 6 (the test)
If you're building right now, ask yourself:

If AI capabilities get 10x better in 6 months, does your product become more valuable — or less?

Cursor bet AI would stay inside the editor. It didn't. The bet collapsed.

## Tweet 7 (what survives)
What survives lifecycle compression:

- Databases
- Auth providers
- Payment infrastructure
- Audit trails

What doesn't survive:

- "Nice UX on top of someone else's API"

Stripe didn't get replaced when mobile payments took off. The UI layers got churned.
