# Blitzy Lessons for Permission Protocol

**Date:** 2026-05-12
**Trigger:** Christopher Harris (Blitzy CRO) rejected PP outreach. Investigation of blitzy.com revealed pricing, positioning, and GTM patterns worth stealing for PP.

**Sources:** blitzy.com homepage, /our_story, /how_it_works, Forbes/BusinessWire $200M round announcement, Reddit pricing thread, Christopher Harris and Brian Elliott LinkedIn posts.

## Headline finding

Blitzy is selling agentic enterprise transformation at consulting-firm prices to regulated industries. **Pilot pricing alone is $50K-$250K per month**, with enterprise plans at $500K-$10M per year. The customers buying this are government, financial services, insurance, and life sciences. PP's natural buyer set overlaps directly.

The implication is that the market for "agentic infrastructure that satisfies regulated enterprise" is already paying real money. PP's job is to be on the buy list when the CCO/CISO procures this stack.

## Six lessons for PP

### 1. The pricing is the positioning

Blitzy tiers (verified from screenshots):
- Explore: $0, scope-bound trial
- Concept Validation: $50K/month, 100K LOC onboarded
- Structured Pilot: $250K/month, 5M LOC onboarded
- Enterprise: $500K+/year, 50M LOC
- Transformation: $10M+/year, custom

**PP implication:** Stop thinking SaaS. Start thinking consulting-firm-replacement. A CCO's budget for "AI agent authorization layer with audit trail that satisfies FDA/SEC/FINRA" is multi-six-figures annually. Anything less signals you don't believe in what you're building.

Target PP pricing:
- Pilot: $25K-$50K
- Enterprise annual: $50K-$500K per customer
- Strategic deployment (Fortune 500): $500K-$2M per year

### 2. Lead with concrete case-study numbers

Blitzy case study schema:
- Lines of code onboarded
- % completed autonomously
- Engineering hours saved

Every case study uses the same three columns. No fluff.

**PP case study schema to build (once Art and Sunil pilots produce data):**
- Agent actions reviewed per month
- % auto-approved (vs human-required)
- Audit trail completeness rate
- Compliance review hours saved
- Time from action to receipt (seconds vs. days)

### 3. Category language as positioning

Blitzy claims: "Pioneering the Agentic SDLC." Not "we build AI tools." A category claim, then leadership of it.

**PP category options (pick one):**
- "The Authorization Layer for Agentic AI"
- "Pioneering the Agentic Authority Layer"
- "Agentic Compliance Infrastructure"
- "Permission Protocol: External Brakes for the Agentic Enterprise"

Recommendation: **"Pioneering the Agentic Authority Layer"** has the architectural ring of Blitzy's claim and stakes a category PP can own. "Authority" is the right word because it captures both authorization and accountability.

### 4. Founder bio as credibility weapon

Brian Elliott bio: Harvard Business School + US Army Ranger + West Point. Sid Pardeshi: 27 patents in Generative AI + 7 years at NVIDIA + Harvard MS/MBA.

**PP implication:** Lead Rodrigo's public bio with operator credibility relevant to the CCO/CFO buyer, not founder generic. If the credentials aren't directly relevant, recruit one advisor whose credentials are:
- Former FINRA examiner
- Former Big 4 audit partner
- Former Fortune 500 CCO
- Former HHS/OCR or SEC official

Even at 0.5-1% equity, the right operator advisor on the team page is a 5x credibility multiplier for the CCO/CFO buyer set.

### 5. CTAs: "Talk to an expert," not "Book a demo"

Every Blitzy page CTA: "Start building" + "Talk to an expert" side by side.

**PP equivalents (when site is ready):**
- "Try the receipt demo" (self-serve, low commitment, like /r/demo)
- "Talk to a compliance engineer" (sales conversation with someone technical, not BDR)

The phrase "Talk to an expert" signals "you'll get someone who knows the architecture, not a sales rep." That posture works for senior buyers.

### 6. Scale moat statement

Blitzy moat: "Understands 100M+ lines of code." It's a system-design claim that competitors can't easily match.

**PP moat candidates:**
- "PP receipts retained for 7+ years to match SEC/FINRA retention. Most agent logging is 90 days."
- "PP enforces 100+ policy primitives per workflow, not allow/deny."
- "PP works across [N] agent platforms: Cursor, Replit, Codex, AutoGPT, custom stacks."

Pick one and make it the moat statement on the website.

## Pricing-model opening Blitzy can't easily counter

Blitzy's pricing is per-million-lines-of-code-generated. That makes their customers' monthly invoices variable. The CFO doing budget math every month will eventually push back.

**PP's natural counter:** Deterministic annual pricing. "Permission Protocol is $X/year for your AI authorization layer, regardless of agent volume." A CFO can budget with certainty.

This is a positioning advantage Blitzy can't easily steal back because their economic model (more agent throughput = more revenue) is structurally tied to usage pricing.

## What not to copy

Blitzy's "80% AI, 20% human" framing works for code generation but does not work for authorization. PP should claim **100% of consequential authorization decisions, with human override on every one**. The Blitzy framing accepts that AI sometimes ships wrong code; PP should never accept that an unauthorized action ships. Different positioning posture.

## What to ignore on the Blitzy site

The "thousands of agents thinking for hours" framing is impressive but technical. The CCO/CFO buyer set doesn't care how many agents run in parallel. They care about audit trail and approval evidence. PP's positioning should not chase the "more agents" narrative; that's a coding-platform metric, not an authorization-layer metric.

## Job board signals (added May 12 PM, post-job-board review)

Reviewed Blitzy's 27 open roles. Six high-signal observations about their org design and GTM motion.

### Signal 1: Operational Deployment is a top-level department

Not a sub-function of Sales or Engineering. Six roles including Managing Director, Digital Transformation, Engineering Manager Client Solutions, Forward Deployed Designer, Forward Deployed Software Engineer (3 locations). This is McKinsey/Accenture delivery structure mapped to enterprise software. Confirms they're selling white-glove engagements, not SaaS deployments.

**PP implication:** Plan for an Operational Deployment function from day one. Rodrigo runs it himself for first 6-12 months. The buyer paying $50-500K/year expects a named partner, not a Zendesk ticket.

### Signal 2: Forward Deployed Engineer is 4-location, 4-headcount

Three Boston metro locations (Cambridge, Burlington, Quincy) plus Bengaluru. Palantir model: cluster FDEs near customer, offshore support tier.

**PP implication:** First post-MVP hire is the founding FDE, not an AE. Someone who can sit with a CCO for 3 days and architect their PP deployment. Makes more revenue than 3 BDRs.

### Signal 3: Field CTO is a Sales role

Field CTO is listed under Sales, not Engineering. Sales department also has: AI Solutions Consultant (x2), Senior AI Solutions Consultant, Enterprise Account Executive, Engagement Manager, Head of Channel/Cloud/PE Partnerships, Enterprise AI Consultant Program. Zero traditional SDR/BDR titles.

**PP implication:** Don't hire SDRs. Don't build outbound demand-gen. Hire first revenue person as Field CTO equivalent: deep compliance/security background, can sit across from Fortune 500 CCO and run discovery without a BDR script.

### Signal 4: Head of Channel, Cloud & PE Partnerships

They're building channel partnerships with hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, GCP) AND with Private Equity firms. PE firms own the portfolio companies that buy enterprise software; the CFO of a PE-backed company answers to the PE operating partner.

**PP implication:** This is a massive leverage point. Add PE Operating Partners as a fifth outreach segment. Specifically people titled "Operating Partner, Technology" or "Portfolio Operations, AI" at Vista, Thoma Bravo, Insight, KKR, Bain Capital portfolio operations. Landing one PE operating partner can mean landing dozens of portfolio companies.

### Signal 5: Engagement Manager + AI Solutions Consultant are consulting titles

Not SaaS titles. Job titles are deliberately positioned to look like McKinsey/Accenture to the buyer.

**PP implication:** Use consulting-grade titles when PP hires:
- Engagement Manager (not Customer Success Manager)
- Field CTO / Solutions Architect (not Sales Engineer)
- Forward Deployed Engineer (not Implementation Specialist)
- Director, Compliance Engagement (not Account Manager)

### Signal 6: India hiring tells the unit economics story

Five of 27 roles in Pune or Bengaluru (~19% offshore), all engineering or operational delivery. At $250K/month pilots, COGS is forward-deployed time. Offshoring the support engineering tier keeps margins enterprise-grade.

**PP implication:** Plan offshore engineering support tier from day one or year one. India or LatAm. Not the FDE quality tier, the underlying operations and SRE work that supports the FDE in the field. This is how PP gets to 70%+ gross margins on $50-500K/year enterprise contracts.

### The most important thing they're NOT hiring

No Compliance Officer. No General Counsel. No Director of Risk. No Head of Trust. Blitzy sells INTO regulated industries but hasn't yet built their own internal compliance brand. **That's the gap PP fills.**

This is the single most important strategic insight from the job board: PP is not competing with Blitzy, PP is the **complementary authorization layer** for Blitzy's regulated customers. A CCO buying Blitzy at $250K/month is already the buyer for PP. PP isn't taking budget from Blitzy; PP sits next to Blitzy in the same procurement cycle.

The reframe: "Blitzy plus PP equals the regulated enterprise's complete agentic stack." Blitzy builds the velocity, PP gates the action.

### Hiring sequence implied by Blitzy job board

After first 3-5 paying pilots, PP's hiring order should be:

1. Founding Field CTO / Solutions Architect (technical + compliance-savvy)
2. Founding Forward Deployed Engineer (can deploy PP in 2 weeks)
3. Operating Partner Network Manager (PE firm relationships)
4. (Optional) Offshore SRE / DevOps engineer for support tier

**Do not hire:** SDR, AE, Customer Success Manager. Not yet. Not in the first 5-10 hires.

PP is already doing 3 of these. The number and the FDE hire are the two missing pieces.
