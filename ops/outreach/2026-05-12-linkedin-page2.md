## v2 changes (post-Brandon reply)

First reply on page 1 (Brandon Looker, Kinter.ai Solutions Engineer) was a polite brush-off. Take-aways applied to page 2 partner-track closers:

- Dropped "Worth a quick exchange?" everywhere (too easy to dodge reflexively).
- New closer on every partner-track message: **"Open to a 15 next week if the framing lands."**
  - "Open to" not "would love to" or "worth a" signals time scarcity by default
  - "Next week" implies you're booked this week
  - "If the framing lands" gives them an out, which raises yes-rates
  - Asserts position without begging
- Routing rule for partner-track: send the CEO/founder first, not the SE/recruiter/CRO. Brandon (SE at Kinter) has no authority to entertain a partnership cold. The Blitzy trio on page 2 should prioritize Brian Elliott first.

## Polite-decline reply template (Brandon-style replies)

When someone politely declines without a counter-question:

> Fair enough, [Name]. We'll be here when [Company]'s enterprise customers start asking the signoff question. Appreciate the quick reply.

Why it works: polite, but plants "inevitable" without arguing. Closes the loop without burning the door. Signals continuity ("we'll be here"), not chasing.

## v1 voice changes from page 1

Page 2 is heavier on AI security founders (Blitzy, Legit, Aikido, Cerbos, Chainguard, Endor Labs, Anaconda) and Chief Compliance Officers / General Counsels. Sophisticated audience. The "I'm betting" framing reads tentative to people who've already built billion-dollar companies in adjacent spaces.

New voice:

- No "I'm betting" anywhere
- Lead with what we're seeing in the market, what keeps coming up, the pattern emerging, not what we suspect
- Use founder-authority phrases: "what keeps coming up", "the pattern we keep seeing", "the gap we keep hitting", "what we hear from buyers"
- Stay humble by asking, not asserting: "does that match what you're seeing?"
- Acknowledge their expertise when it earns the position ("you've sat in this seat", "Cerbos is closer to this than most")
- Use plural pronouns where it implies a team and active customer conversations ("we keep hearing", "we keep running into")

## Voice patterns to keep

- "What keeps coming up..."
- "The pattern we keep running into..."
- "We keep hearing buyers ask..."
- "[Company] is closer to this than most..." (when sincere)
- "Does this match what you're seeing?"

## Voice patterns to avoid

- "I'm betting..."
- "I think..."
- "I believe..."
- "I'm curious..." (too tentative for senior buyers)
- Any superlative without context ("the best", "the only", "the most important")
