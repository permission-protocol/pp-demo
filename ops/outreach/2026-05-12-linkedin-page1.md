## Approach

Diagnostic question plus product line. Around 45 words. No links, no Calendly. Asks for a reaction, not a meeting.

Variation rules to avoid sounding AI-written:
- No em dashes anywhere
- Vary the opener (some "thanks for connecting", some "TFC", some lead with the hook)
- Vary the question stem (some "quick yes/no", some "curious", some direct)
- Vary the product line (a few different phrasings of the same thing)
- One personalization token max per message
- Use straight quotes, not curly quotes
- Keep contractions
- Some sentences short, some longer, some fragments

Send rate: 30 to 40 a day, not all 97. LinkedIn throttles, and your reply pipeline matters more than blast volume.

## Stress-test changes from v1

1. Removed all em dashes (was the biggest AI tell)
2. Removed the identical closing line that repeated across all 25 messages

7. First-person voice with some opinion attached, not neutral reporting

If they say "yes, this is real":

> Helpful. Which action is on your mind the most: approvals, payments, customer-data access, deploys, or workflow triggers?

If they say "still too early":

> Fair. Are the agents in your stack still mostly read-only and drafting, or can any of them call tools that change state?

If they say "tell me more":

> Permission Protocol blocks high-risk AI actions until the right human or policy signs the exact action, then issues a tamper-evident receipt. Worth a 15-minute teardown this week? Sharp feedback over sales pitch.

## Do not include in first message

- Calendly or calendar links
- Demo offers
- "Cryptographically signed" (engineer-speak when writing to CFOs)
- "Pilot cohort" framing (premature, makes you sound like you're recruiting them)
- Em dashes
- "Exactly that" or any phrase repeated across multiple messages
