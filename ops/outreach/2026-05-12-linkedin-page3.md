## Voice for compliance audience (v2, post-Steven)

First draft of page 3 stacked too much in each message: setup clause + diagnostic + meta-question. Steven Cervantes (ex-FINRA Senior Regulator) replied "can you rephrase the question?", signal that the compound structure was asking him to parse two things at once. The rephrase that worked was a single clean diagnostic with vertical-specific actions named.

New rule for compliance/CCO/GC messages on page 3:

- **One clean diagnostic, not setup-plus-diagnostic.** Skip "the pattern we keep hearing is X... is Y on your roadmap?". Just ask the diagnostic.
- **Name the actual vertical-specific actions.** For RIAs that's "trade, comm, fee change". For broker-dealers it's "investor communications, KYC, transaction approvals". For SOX it's "control testing, journal entries, vendor changes". Generic phrases like "client-impacting decisions" or "customer workflows" don't earn the response.
- **Lead with the question, end with one closing nudge.** Not two-part questions.
- **"I'm finding most firms can't"** is a soft observation that earns disagreement permission (Steven rephrase). Better than "the pattern we keep hearing" for senior regulators.

For people who've already publicly posted PP-aligned content (James McNeely on NSA/CISA agentic AI white paper, Lewis Bertolucci on MCP RCE flaw), reference the piece by name. That's a real signal, not a fake "loved your post" hook.

## Voice patterns to keep using

- "What keeps coming up..."
- "The pattern we keep running into..."
- "We keep hearing buyers ask..."
- "[Company] is closer to this than most..." (when sincere)
- "The regulator/auditor question is..."
- "Does this match what you're seeing?"

## Voice patterns to avoid

- "I'm betting..."
- "I think..." / "I believe..."
- "I'm curious..." (too tentative)
- "Worth a quick exchange?" (replaced with "Open to a 15 next week if the framing lands.")
- Any superlative without context
- Fake hook openers ("Loved your post...")
