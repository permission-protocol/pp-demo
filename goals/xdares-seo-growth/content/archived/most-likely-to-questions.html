How to Play Most Likely To

Classic Rules

  1. Gather in a circle (3+ players works best)
  2. One person reads a "Most Likely To..." question
  3. On the count of 3, everyone points at who they think fits best
  4. The person with the most fingers pointed at them "wins" (or loses, depending on the question)
  5. Optional: They take a drink or do a dare

Drinking Game Version

The person most voted takes one sip for every finger pointed at them. With 6 friends pointing? That's 6 sips. Choose your friends wisely.

Dare Variant

Skip the drinks. Whoever gets the most votes has to complete a dare chosen by the group. Escalate throughout the night for maximum chaos.

🎯 Pro Tips for Maximum Fun

Set the Vibe

  • Start tame, go wild: Begin with clean questions and escalate as people loosen up
  • Read the room: Not every group is ready for the spicy section
  • No explanations required: The fun is in the instant judgments

Variations to Try

  • Speed Round: 3 seconds to point, no hesitation allowed
  • Story Time: Whoever gets voted has to explain WHY they're "most likely"
  • Reverse: Vote for who's LEAST likely (often more revealing)
  • Anonymous: Write names on paper, reveal results dramatically

Combine With Other Games

Most Likely To pairs perfectly with:

  • Never Have I Ever — Reveal who's actually done the thing
  • Truth or Dare — Add consequences
  • Kings Cup — Add as a rule card

Frequently Asked Questions

How many people do you need to play Most Likely To?

You need at least 3 people, but the game is best with 5-10 players. Larger groups mean more potential for surprising votes and contested results.

What happens if there's a tie in Most Likely To?

When there's a tie, both people drink or complete the dare. Some groups add a "tiebreaker question" where the tied players have to justify why the OTHER person deserves the vote more.

Can you point at yourself in Most Likely To?

Yes! Self-votes count. Some questions practically demand self-nomination. A confident self-vote can be hilarious — or terrifying, depending on the question.

What's the difference between Most Likely To and Never Have I Ever?

Most Likely To is about predictions — who's likely to do something. Never Have I Ever is about confessions — who's already done something. They pair perfectly together!

Is Most Likely To a drinking game?

It can be! The drinking version has the "winner" (person with most votes) take one sip per finger pointed at them. But it works just as well without alcohol — use dares, points, or just play for laughs.
