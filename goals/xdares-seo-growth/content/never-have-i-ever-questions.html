How to Play Never Have I Ever

If you somehow made it through college without playing this game, here's the deal:

Basic Rules

  1. Everyone starts with 10 fingers up (or a full drink)
  2. One person says "Never have I ever..." followed by something they haven't done
  3. Anyone who HAS done that thing puts a finger down (or drinks)
  4. First person to lose all fingers loses—or wins, depending on your perspective
  5. Optional: Anyone who drinks must share the story

The Strategic Element

Here's where it gets interesting: if you're the only one who puts a finger down, everyone knows your secret. Smart players craft questions that they suspect will catch multiple people—spreading the shame around.

Even smarter players target specific people: "Never have I ever made out with someone at Sarah's birthday party last year" is perfectly valid if you want to expose someone.

Pro Tips for Better Games

1. Control the Pace

Start innocent, escalate gradually. Don't lead with "Never have I ever had a threesome" when half the room hasn't finished their first drink.

2. Require Stories

The best part isn't the confession—it's the story behind it. Make anyone who drinks share at least a sentence. "Wait, WHEN did this happen?!" is where the gold is.

3. Target Strategically

If you know someone has a secret, craft a question that exposes them without being obvious. The subtle call-out is an art form.

4. Have a Mix Ready

Keep questions from different categories on deck. If things get too heavy, pivot to funny. If energy is low, go spicy.

5. Know When to Stop

If someone looks genuinely uncomfortable, move on. It's a game, not an interrogation. The goal is fun, not trauma.

Game Variations

Reverse Never Have I Ever

Say something you HAVE done. Only people who've also done it drink. Bonus: you admit your own secret every turn.

Never Have I Ever Speed Round

Set a timer for 5 minutes. Rapid-fire questions, no stories, just confessions. Most fingers down at the end loses.

Targeted Never Have I Ever

Go around the circle where each question must be about the person to your left. Forces creativity and strategic thinking.

Never Have I Ever + Dare

If only ONE person drinks on a question, they must complete a dare instead of (or in addition to) drinking. Combine it with truth or dare drinking game rules for maximum chaos.

Confessional Mode

No drinks/fingers—just go around answering "have you ever" questions honestly. Better for deeper conversations and getting to know people.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you play Never Have I Ever?

Everyone holds up 10 fingers (or has a drink ready). One person says "Never have I ever..." followed by something they haven't done. Anyone who HAS done that thing puts a finger down (or takes a drink). The first person to run out of fingers loses—or wins, depending on how you look at it!

What are good Never Have I Ever questions for adults?

Good adult Never Have I Ever questions include: "Never have I ever had a one-night stand," "Never have I ever been kicked out of a bar," "Never have I ever lied to get out of work," "Never have I ever sent a risky text to the wrong person," and "Never have I ever had a crush on a friend's partner."

What's the difference between Never Have I Ever and Truth or Dare?

Never Have I Ever focuses on confessing past experiences—you reveal what you have or haven't done. Truth or Dare adds an action element: you either answer a personal question (truth) OR complete a challenge (dare). Never Have I Ever is purely about confession; Truth or Dare includes real-time challenges. Try our truth or dare for adults for the full experience.

How do you make Never Have I Ever more interesting?

To make Never Have I Ever more interesting: 1) Require stories from anyone who puts a finger down, 2) Add a drinking element, 3) Include spicier questions as the game progresses, 4) Play 'reverse' where you try to get others OUT by targeting their experiences, 5) Add consequences for the person who loses first.

Is Never Have I Ever appropriate for teens?

Yes! Never Have I Ever can be completely appropriate for teens when you stick to age-appropriate questions about school, friendships, hobbies, and embarrassing moments. Skip alcohol references and adult content. Clean versions work great for sleepovers, youth groups, and family game nights. Check our teen-friendly truth or dare for more ideas.

How many questions should you have for Never Have I Ever?

Plan for 30-50 questions for a solid game session. With 10 fingers, most games last 20-40 questions before someone loses. Having extras ensures variety and lets you skip questions that don't land. For parties, prepare 50+ questions so you can replay without repetition.
