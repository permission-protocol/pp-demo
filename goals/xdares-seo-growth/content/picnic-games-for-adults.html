Picnic Games for Adults: 50+ Fun Activities & Drinking Games

There's nothing quite like a sunny afternoon in the park with a basket full of food and a cooler full of drinks. But once the sandwiches are gone, what's next? Instead of just lounging on the blanket, why not bring some energy to the gathering with picnic games for adults?

In this guide, we've compiled over 50 of the best outdoor activities ranging from high-energy lawn games to low-key conversation starters and park-friendly drinking games. Whether you're at a local park, a beach, or your own backyard, these games will ensure your picnic is one for the books. Many of these are excellent outdoor lawn games that work for any gathering.

Active Lawn Games (The Classics)

These games require a bit of space and equipment but are always a hit at any outdoor event.

Cornhole

The undisputed king of lawn games. Toss bean bags into the holes for points. Pro Tip: Focus on the flat slide rather than a high arc for better control on the board.

Kubb

A Swedish "Viking" game where you knock over wooden blocks by tossing sticks. It's like chess but with more throwing. Strategy: Group your fallen blocks close together when tossing them back to make them easier to knock down in one go.

Spikeball

A high-energy 2v2 game that's perfect for the athletic group in the park. Pro Tip: Stay on your toes and move around the net—360-degree play is what makes Spikeball unique.

Bocce Ball

A slower-paced, strategic game of getting your balls closest to the "pallino." Strategy: Use your opponent's balls as backstops or knock them away if they're too close to the target.

Giant Jenga

The classic block-stacking game but oversized. High stakes when it falls! For a boozy twist, check out our Drunk Jenga rules to add specific dares to the blocks.

No-Equipment Picnic Games

Forgot to pack the Spikeball set? No problem. These games only require people and a little creativity.

Two Truths and a Lie

Perfect for getting to know new friends on the picnic blanket. Pro Tip: Pick a lie that's slightly mundane so it blends in with your truths. Check out our Two Truths and a Lie guide for ideas.

Charades

Classic and hilarious, especially after a few drinks. Pro Tip: Establish clear signals for "movie," "book," or "song" before you start.

Mafia / Werewolf

A social deduction game that works great with a large group sitting in a circle. Strategy: As a villager, pay close attention to who is quick to accuse others without evidence.

Categories

Pick a category (e.g., "Types of Cheese") and go around the circle. First to stall or repeat drinks. This is a staple in many drinking games for parties.

Park-Friendly Drinking Games

If your local park allows alcohol (check the rules first!), these games are perfect for a boozy afternoon.

Giant Beer Pong

Use buckets and a volleyball for a "Giant Beer Pong" experience. It's much easier to play on grass than a standard table. Check our Beer Pong variations for more ways to play.

Flip Cup

Easy to set up on a picnic table or even a cooler. Pro Tip: Tap the table before you flip—it's tradition! For tournament ideas, see our Flip Cup tournament guide.

Picnic Blanket Kings

Play the classic Kings Cup, but use a portable speaker to announce the rules. We have a complete Kings Cup rule guide if you forget what a 7 or 10 means.

Dizzy Bat

Chug a beer out of a hollow plastic bat, spin around with your head on the bat 10 times, and try to hit the empty can. Pro Tip: Have a friend standing nearby to catch you if you stumble!

Tips for a Successful Game Picnic

  • Check Park Rules: Always verify the local ordinances regarding alcohol, noise, and sports equipment.
  • Sun Protection: Playing games means you'll be out in the sun longer than you think. Pack extra sunscreen!
  • Hydration: Balance the adult beverages with plenty of water.
  • Leave No Trace: Clean up all game equipment and trash before you head home.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: What are the best picnic games for small groups?

A: For 2-4 people, Bocce Ball, Cornhole, and conversation games like "Would You Rather" are ideal.

Q: Can we play drinking games in public parks?

A: This depends entirely on local laws. Many parks allow beer and wine but prohibit glass containers or hard liquor. Always check signs and local government websites.

Q: What if it's too windy for Frisbee?

A: Switch to heavier games like Kubb or Cornhole, or stick to blanket games that don't rely on the wind.
