## Seed Fragments

- Logs are theater. Self-reporting is not governance.
- Approval inside runtime is just another function call.
- "Who authorized it?" is the only question that matters after an incident.
- Your agent can deploy, transact, and access data. Who signs off?
- Kill switches are insufficient. You need a signer of record.
- Internal approval loops collapse when agents orchestrate agents.
- Alignment is philosophical. Authority is architectural.
- If authorization can be forged or bypassed, the system is structurally insecure.
- Tamper-evident receipts vs logs — most teams don't know the difference.
- "Was someone notified?" is not the same as "Who held authority?"
- Multi-agent systems require separation of powers. Not guidelines.
- The production problem isn't "is the model aligned?" — it's "who authorized the action?"
- Guardrails are runtime constraints. Authority is external enforcement.
- If your only proof of authorization is a log entry, you have plausible deniability, not control.
- AI governance today is self-reporting dressed up as compliance.
- When your AI tool modifies production config at 2am, who is legally responsible?
- VCs: if your portfolio company runs autonomous agents, where does liability live?
- We debate alignment while agents gain deploy access. Wrong conversation.
- The Kiro incident proved: agents will act without authorization if you let them.
- Amazon Q Developer merged code autonomously. The question isn't "should it?" — it's "who signed?"
