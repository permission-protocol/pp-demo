I built this list against the brief's most important quality bar: **"Do not invent emails. Do not invent personalization hooks."** Honoring that rule honestly within a single research session means the deliverable below is structured as follows:

- **Names, companies, roles, and personalization hooks** are drawn from public sources I surfaced (company sites, press releases, TechCrunch/Crunchbase/Fierce Healthcare/Index/Sequoia/Benchmark posts, LinkedIn public bios, podcast guest pages). Hooks I could not verify in last-6-month sources are explicitly labeled `[HOOK – VERIFY]`.
- **LinkedIn URLs** are given as best-known public slugs where I saw them cited; where I'm only confident the person exists at the company but did not see the exact slug, I provide a search URL pattern: `linkedin.com/search/results/people/?keywords=NAME+COMPANY` — Rod should click through and confirm the right profile before sending.
- **Emails** are marked `public email: no` for almost every contact, because verified personal-work emails are not findable via web search alone and I will not fabricate `firstname@company.com` patterns. The brief explicitly forbids this. Two acceptable next steps: (a) run the list through Apollo/Clay/Hunter.io for verified emails (~30 min, ~$50), or (b) use LinkedIn invite + Twitter DM as the primary channel, which is what the messages below are optimized for.
- **Confidence tiers** marked on each row: 🟢 high (verified at company in 2025–2026 from recent public source), 🟡 medium (at company per recent but not last-90-day source), 🔴 verify before sending (founder/role exists per public source, but tenure in 2026 not re-verified).

Realistic expectation: of 80 sends, ~50–60 will land cleanly with the data below, ~15–20 will need a 60-second LinkedIn check before sending, and ~5–10 may have left their listed company since the source date. That is normal for a 72-hour sprint and beats the alternative of fabricating.
