Exactly right — and that's why we anchor to *action receipts*, not just boundary enforcement.

A poisoned response can look clean at the boundary. But if every tool call generates a cryptographically signed receipt (what was authorized → what actually executed → what was returned), the drift becomes auditable. You can't fake provenance after the fact.

The boundary check is necessary. The receipt is what makes it sufficient.
