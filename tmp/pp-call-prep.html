Permission Protocol — Call Prep

The one sentence
We make sure no AI agent can take a high-stakes action — like deploying code or moving money — without a human signing off first.
30-second version
AI agents are starting to deploy code, access customer data, trigger payments — real actions with real consequences. Right now, most of them authorize themselves. There's no external check.

We're the external signer. Before a risky action executes, it has to be approved by the right human. If it's not approved, it gets rejected. If it is, you get a cryptographic receipt proving who authorized what.

Words

The closing question

"Do your agents currently do anything in production that nobody explicitly authorized?"
Then shut up. Let them answer. Whatever they say is your next talking point.

Handle objections

"How is this different from GitHub branch protection?"
Branch protection is a yes/no gate. We capture who approved it, what exactly they approved, and give you a tamper-evident receipt you can verify later.
"Can't we just use Slack approvals?"
Slack approvals are ephemeral. No cryptographic proof, no binding to the exact action. We bind the approval to the specific payload.
"We have audit logs already."
Audit logs tell you what happened after. We require authorization before. The action doesn't execute without the receipt.
"Who's using this?"
We're in early access. A handful of teams run it on deploy workflows. We're looking for design partners who want to shape the product.

Mindset

  • You're not selling PP. You're asking if the problem is real.
  • Find the pain, shut up. Let silence do the work.
  • Don't fake traction. "Early but real" beats "everyone loves us."
  • If they don't have the pain, that's fine. "Thanks — can I check back in 3 months?"
  • Demo > slides. Always share screen over deck.
